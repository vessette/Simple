2Challenges

  • Lead offers may change with nobody checking.safety The system can re-score leads every 6 hours, switch their offer, and add them to a second email campaign. People who unsubscribed may be included. Ali confirmed it's running.
  • Nothing records who chose a lead's offer. So a person's routing choice can't be protected or enforced yet. This needs a platform change, and sales can't file the request against the platform domain itself.
  • Vick's rules aren't defined yet. Pass marks, what makes a lead warm, what always stays with a person, and the correct price and dates all need Roselyn's answers. Until then every task stays a draft.
  • The platform disagrees on when a call counts as missed. The goal says 15 minutes, the spec says 30 minutes, and the code that runs waits 2 hours. Vick won't pick one until Roselyn does.
  • Two older agents may be acting without approval. They retry failed emails and mark week-old errors as resolved automatically. A sequence that has stopped sending also scores 100. Whether these agents run is unknown.
  • Expired approval requests are approved automatically. A platform job approves any pending request once its expiry passes. Vick's packets carry no expiry, but the platform rule needs changing before outreach approvals go through it.
  • The platform's digest is fragile. If one source fails, no digest goes out at all; it can send twice; and it follows the server's clock, so a 7 AM digest may arrive at 1 AM Central.
  • Payments are marked paid without checking they settled. A returned payment never frees the seat or removes access, and the job that would take it out of revenue isn't running. Ali decided only settled payments count, and a reversal drops the student to the free preview tier with their login kept. The fix has to keep checking a payment that's waiting to settle, or someone who paid stays locked out.
  • My payment change request had three mistakes. Kes found them: I missed the 30-minute job, the amount floor is $100 not $50, and the platform has several rules for what counts as paid, not one. Quincy has since counted five. My fix could also have locked out someone who really paid. The brief is corrected. Ali has since found the 30-minute job is off, so the revenue problem is as big as I first said.
  • No refund terms are approved, and the platform has three versions. One is labelled approved, so another agent could quote it. A refund also leaves paid access and the seat in place. Taiwo and Ali were asked on 11 November which version is final.
  • A cancellation with no payment schedule reports success silently. That's right for someone who pays by hand, but nobody is told the payment provider was never touched. Members who are past due also can't cancel from the portal.
  • A free seat can be counted the wrong way in revenue. Granting one marks the person as paid. If they were already paying, or the seat was later taken back, the revenue page can count them wrongly. The audit log shows who granted a seat and when, but nobody is asked why.
  • The missed-payment job has gaps of its own. It counts authorised payments as collected, its check against shared customer records covers only one of its two match paths, and its time window depends on a date format nobody has confirmed in production.
  • "Revenue" means two different things on the platform. The platform says net revenue can't be computed yet, but the payments page shows a "net" figure anyway. And "collected" holds one amount per member, so a member who has paid six times shows one payment.
  • Many code references in the task specs point at the wrong place. Anyone checking Vick's evidence would be sent to the wrong code. A note with the results is ready for Ali and Kes.
  • There's nowhere in the platform for the manager to approve Vick's work. A decision queue for Roselyn is one of Vick's planned tasks, and the platform has no code for it yet.
